🌾 Crop Information

🌾 Sorghum

Scientific name: Sorghum bicolor

Sorghum is an important small-grain cereal valued for its ability to tolerate high temperatures and periods of limited rainfall. Its deep root system and ability to slow growth during dry conditions help it survive challenging growing environments.

Common uses

The grain is processed into flour and traditional foods such as sadza and porridge. It is also used in beverages and livestock feed.

Farming consideration

Farmers should select varieties suited to their agro-ecological region, maturity requirements and intended grain use.

🌱 Pearl Millet

Scientific name: Pennisetum glaucum

Pearl millet is a hardy cereal particularly valuable in hot, dry farming environments. It is highly tolerant of drought and can cope with mid-season dry spells better than many other cereal crops.

Common uses

Its grain is processed into meal, flour and porridge. It contributes to household food security and can be stored for extended periods when properly dried and protected.

Farming consideration

Pearl millet is particularly useful in areas experiencing unreliable rainfall. Well-drained soils and suitable locally adapted varieties support successful production.

🌾 Finger Millet

Scientific name: Eleusine coracana

Finger millet is a nutritious traditional cereal cultivated for its small grains. It is valued for its dietary fibre, iron and calcium content and plays an important role in traditional food systems.

Common uses

The grain is processed into flour, porridge and other traditional foods. Its good storage potential also makes it valuable for household food security.

Farming consideration

Finger millet generally requires more water than sorghum and pearl millet. It performs best in fertile, well-drained soils with appropriate moisture management.
